# Methods

**Status:** Draft v0.1 — not yet frozen. Once frozen (v1.0), this document is published before any entries, and every later change is logged in the changelog at the bottom with its reason.

This document defines what the catalog records, how candidates are found, how evidence is assessed, and how entries are verified. It is written so that a reader who disagrees with any conclusion can see exactly how that conclusion was reached and check every step without trusting the authors.

---

## 1. Purpose

### 1.1 What this project is

A searchable evidence base documenting significant interpretive choices in the New World Translation of the Holy Scriptures (NWT), their linguistic and textual foundations, their documented relationship to Watch Tower teaching, and comparable choices in other translations.

### 1.2 What this project is not

- It is not an attempt to prove the NWT is a "bad" translation. All translation involves interpretation; that is the starting point, not the finding.
- It does not claim to know the private motives of any translator. It records what was published: renderings, stated rationales, and doctrinal uses.
- It does not assume that the majority rendering is correct. How many translations agree is evidence about precedent, not about truth.

### 1.3 The research question

> Where does the NWT resolve linguistic, grammatical or textual ambiguity in ways that are unusual, internally inconsistent, or documented by Watch Tower publications as supporting a distinctive doctrine — and how do other translations handle the same places?

### 1.4 Scope

- **Target:** the whole Bible, all NWT editions.
- **Pace check:** a pilot (§14) measures the real rate in chapters per day. If the full scope is not sustainable, Phase I is limited to the Christian Greek Scriptures (New Testament) and the Hebrew Scriptures become Phase II.
- Any scope change is recorded in the changelog.

## 2. Principles

1. **Three layers, never mixed.** Every entry separates facts (Layer 1), documented positions (Layer 2) and the author's commentary (Layer 3). See §3.
2. **No motive language.** Entries never say a rendering was chosen *in order to* support a doctrine. They state the rendering, its linguistic support, its internal consistency, and what Watch Tower has published about it. Where Watch Tower itself has tied a rendering to doctrine, that is recorded as a fact with a citation (the R-scale, §7.4).
3. **Symmetry.** The same rubric is applied to other translations. A standing set of control entries (§6.5) documents interpretive choices in non-JW translations, including cases where the NWT comes out ahead.
4. **Strongest case first.** Every assessed entry states the strongest case *for* the NWT rendering, in terms its defenders would accept, before any assessment.
5. **Frequency is not correctness.** Agreement among translations is reported as a count, never as proof.
6. **Traceability.** Every factual claim links to a checkable source or to a reproducible query over open data. Counts and ratios are generated by code, never written by hand or by AI.
7. **Disclosure.** The author's background and the use of AI are stated openly (§15).

## 3. The three layers

### Layer 1 — Facts

Things anyone can check without trusting the authors:

- The source text (Greek, Hebrew, Aramaic) and which edition of it
- Whether that matches the NWT's stated base text
- What each NWT edition says at the verse
- What the comparison translations say
- What standard lexicons and grammars list
- Occurrence counts and consistency ratios (generated)
- What Watch Tower publications have said about the verse or rendering. That a publication said something is a fact; whether the claim is true is not.

### Layer 2 — Positions

A documented map of the debate:

- Named arguments by named people and organizations (Watch Tower, critics, defenders, academics), each with sources
- The strongest case for and against the NWT rendering
- A description of the scholarly landscape: consensus, majority/minority, or genuinely open
- How other translations handle the same issue

Layer 2 describes who argues what. It does not adjudicate.

### Layer 3 — Commentary

The author's own analysis, clearly labeled, dated, and revisable. Commentary may draw conclusions but may not introduce facts that aren't in Layer 1.

On the site, the three layers are visually distinct so a reader always knows which kind of statement they are reading.

## 4. Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Base text** | The source-language text a translation states it follows. |
| **Rendering** | How a translation expresses a word, phrase or construction at one verse. |
| **Phenomenon** | A pattern with many instances, e.g. "Jehovah in the Christian Greek Scriptures" or "rendering of *proskyneō*." Analysed once. |
| **Instance** | One occurrence of a phenomenon at one verse. Stored as a generated data row. |
| **Entry** | A single verse (or passage) analysed on its own. |
| **Candidate** | A verse flagged by any discovery stream, not yet assessed. |
| **Control entry** | An entry documenting an interpretive choice in a non-NWT translation, assessed with the same rubric. |
| **Peculiarity log** | Record of NWT renderings that meet a linguistic trigger but have no doctrinal link. Kept as control data showing how often the NWT is unusual for reasons unrelated to doctrine (e.g., its concordance policy). |
| **Morphological gloss** | A word-by-word gloss reflecting form (not a translation). Paired with a note on contextual semantic range. |

The word "mainstream" is not used. Translations are described by tradition, date, translation philosophy and publisher.

## 5. Texts

### 5.1 NWT editions tracked

Each edition is a separate data point. Edition changes are among the most objective data in the catalog.

| Edition | Notes |
|---|---|
| 1950 | Christian Greek Scriptures |
| 1953–1960 | Hebrew Scriptures, in volumes |
| 1961 | First one-volume complete edition |
| 1969 / 1985 | *Kingdom Interlinear Translation* (Watch Tower's own word-for-word glosses) |
| 1970 | Revision |
| 1971 | Revision with footnotes |
| 1981 | Revision |
| 1984 | Revision, and the *Reference Bible* (footnotes, appendices) |
| 2013 | Revision |

Verify the details of each edition against the publications themselves before relying on this table (see §17).

### 5.2 Base text policy

- The NWT's New Testament states Westcott–Hort (1881) as its base; the 2013 appendix refers to other critical texts as well. The Hebrew Scriptures state BHK and later BHS. (Verify wording, §17.)
- Every entry records whether a difference is **textual** (a different base text, rendered faithfully) or **translational** (the same base, rendered differently). A textual difference is never counted as a translation choice.
- Where critical editions differ (Westcott–Hort, NA28/UBS5, SBLGNT, Byzantine, Textus Receptus), the entry records which reading each translation follows.

### 5.3 Comparison panel

Chosen to span translation philosophies and traditions, not popularity. The panel is fixed at v1.0 and changes only through the changelog.

| Philosophy | Translations |
|---|---|
| Formal | ASV, YLT, NASB, ESV |
| Mediating | NRSVue, BSB, NET, CSB |
| Functional | NIV, NLT |
| Jewish (Hebrew Scriptures) | JPS 1917, NJPS |
| Catholic | NABRE |
| Historical | KJV |

Open-licensed texts (ASV, YLT, BSB, WEB, KJV, JPS 1917) may be used in bulk by scripts. All others are looked up and quoted per verse only.

### 5.4 Source-language data

- STEPBible TAGNT / TAHOT: lemmas, morphology (CC BY)
- Macula Greek / Hebrew: syntax trees for construction queries (CC BY 4.0)
- A digital Westcott–Hort text (license to verify)
- ETCBC BHSA via Text-Fabric for in-depth Hebrew queries

Each dataset's license is recorded in `data/LICENSES.md` before use.

## 6. Discovery

Candidates come from independent streams, so the catalog isn't limited to what critics already found. Each candidate records which stream found it.

### 6.1 Streams

| # | Stream | Method |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Doctrine trace** | Enumerate distinctive Watch Tower doctrines from Watch Tower's own reference works (*Reasoning from the Scriptures*, *Insight on the Scriptures*, NWT appendices and footnotes). Every verse they cite becomes a candidate. |
| B | **Chapter sweep** | Every chapter is read against the source text and the comparison panel. The sweeper records only *notable / not notable / uncertain* and why, without seeing other streams' results. |
| C | **Generated tables** | For each lemma and construction on the list (§6.2), code lists every occurrence and the NWT rendering of the relevant word or phrase. Splits in rendering are candidates. |
| D | **Edition diffs** | Every change between NWT editions at a verse is a candidate, dated. |
| E | **Divergence triage** | Where legally obtainable text allows, an automated score flags verses where the NWT sits far from the panel. Used only for triage. |
| F | **Recall check (last)** | Published lists by critics and defenders are compared against the catalog. Anything missed is reviewed and logged as found by stream F. |

Stream F runs last so that critics' lists don't set the categories.

### 6.2 Starting lemma and construction list

A starting list, expanded as streams B, C and E surface new items. Each addition is logged.

- **Greek lemmas:** *kyrios*, *theos*, *proskyneō*, *stauros*, *xylon*, *pneuma*, *psychē*, *hadēs*, *geenna*, *tartaroō*, *parousia*, *aiōn*, *aiōnios*, *kolasis*, *monogenēs*, *prōtotokos*, *archē*, *pisteuō eis*, *ekklēsia*
- **Hebrew lemmas:** YHWH, *elohim*, *nephesh*, *sheol*, *ruach*, *olam*, *qanah*
- **Constructions:** anarthrous predicate nominatives; article–substantive–*kai*–substantive (Granville Sharp); vocative vs nominative *ho theos*; supplied words (bracketed or not); punctuation and verse segmentation; genitive relationships in Christological titles; *ego eimi* with and without predicate
- **Watch Tower–distinctive renderings** (ecclesiology, eschatology, practice): e.g. "faithful and discreet slave," "presence," "conclusion of the system of things," "from house to house," "abstain from blood," disfellowshipping language

### 6.3 Coverage audit

Every chapter has a coverage record showing which streams have processed it. The catalog must be able to answer "why isn't this verse included?" with a recorded reason, not "we didn't notice."

### 6.4 Peculiarity log

NWT renderings that meet a trigger (§7.1) but have no doctrinal link go to the peculiarity log instead of the catalog. The log is published as control data.

### 6.5 Control entries

A fixed set of 30–50 control entries, chosen before assessment, documents contested choices in other translations with the same rubric. Examples of the kind intended: rendering of *sarx*, *paradoseis*, Romans 16:7, Isaiah 7:14, Romans 9:5 punctuation, and variants documented as theologically motivated in the manuscript tradition.

## 7. Assessment

There is no single verdict. Each entry is scored on separate dimensions, and the site shows all of them.

### 7.1 Inclusion: triggers

An entry is included if it meets at least one trigger **and** the doctrinal link (§7.2).

| Code | Trigger |
|---|---|
| T1 | **Lexical outlier:** the sense used is not listed in standard lexicons (BDAG, LSJ; HALOT, DCH), or is listed only as marginal. |
| T2 | **Syntactic outlier:** the construal is rejected or marked as a minority view in standard grammars (BDF, Smyth, Wallace, Robertson; GKC, Joüon–Muraoka). |
| T3 | **Textual:** words added or omitted without support in the NWT's own stated base text. Bracketed insertions qualify but are marked as disclosed. |
| T4 | **Internal inconsistency:** the same lemma or construction in comparable contexts is rendered differently, and the split is quantified. |
| T5 | **Segmentation or punctuation:** a choice where the source is genuinely ambiguous. |
| T6 | **Edition change:** a change between NWT editions. |

**Exclusions:** renderings shared by at least two panel translations from different traditions (these may still appear as context); purely stylistic choices; faithful renderings of a different base text (recorded as textual, not translational).

### 7.2 Doctrinal link

The doctrinal link (D) is met when Watch Tower literature cites the rendering in support of a distinctive doctrine, or when critics cite the alternative rendering against one. Its strength is scored by the R-scale (§7.4).

### 7.3 Dimensions

**L — Linguistic support**

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| L1 | **Standard:** primary sense in a standard lexicon or majority construal in standard grammars, and found in at least two non-JW translations from different traditions. |
| L2 | **Minority but attested:** supported by at least one standard reference work, a peer-reviewed argument by a non-JW-affiliated scholar, or a non-JW translation's text or margin. |
| L3 | **Unsupported:** defended only by JW-affiliated sources or by the NWT itself. |

**T — Textual status:** follows its stated base text / departs from it / disclosed insertion (bracketed) / undisclosed insertion.

**C — Internal consistency:** a generated ratio, reported as numbers, e.g. "rendered 'worship' in 0 of N instances with Jesus as object; M of K with God as object." No adjectives.

**R — Documented doctrinal link** (§7.4)

**Confidence:** high (reference works agree), medium (they split), low (evidence thin or question genuinely open).

### 7.4 The R-scale

Measures how strongly Watch Tower's own publications connect the rendering to doctrine. Every level requires a citation.

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| R0 | No published Watch Tower rationale or doctrinal use found. |
| R1 | Covered only by a stated general translation policy (e.g., the concordance principle). |
| R2 | A verse-specific rationale is published (appendix, footnote, article). |
| R3 | Watch Tower literature uses this rendering as proof of a doctrine. |
| R4 | Watch Tower explicitly ties the choice, or a change in it, to doctrine. |

A declared connection is a fact. An undeclared motive is never inferred.

### 7.5 Display labels

The site may summarize entries with plain-language labels derived from the dimensions, never with the words "biased," "dishonest," "deceptive" or similar. For example: *"Minority rendering (L2), follows base text, internally inconsistent (C: 0/14 vs 12/15), used as proof-text (R3)."*

## 8. Evidence standards

### 8.1 Source hierarchy

1. **Primary texts:** manuscripts and critical editions, NWT editions, Watch Tower publications (for what Watch Tower says)
2. **Standard reference works:** lexicons and grammars (§7.1)
3. **Peer-reviewed scholarship:** journals and academic presses
4. **Other scholarship:** books by credentialed authors outside academic presses
5. **Positions:** apologetic works on any side, JW or critical — reported in Layer 2 as positions, never relied on as evidence
6. **Leads:** websites, forums, videos — used only to find candidates, never cited as evidence

### 8.2 Rules

- Every assessed entry needs at least two independent sources, at least one of them non-apologetic.
- Reference works are cited to the page or entry.
- **No citation is published until a person has seen the source.** Citations an agent could not verify are marked `unverified` and block the entry from becoming verified.
- "Scholars agree" is written only when a consensus is documented. Otherwise positions are listed by name.
- Paraphrase by default. Quotations are short and cited.

## 9. Data model

### 9.1 Repository layout

```
content/
  entries/<book>/<chapter>-<verse>.md    # single-verse entries
  phenomena/<slug>.md                    # pattern analyses
  controls/<slug>.md                     # control entries
  peculiarities/<book>.yaml              # peculiarity log
data/
  bibliography.json                      # CSL-JSON, exported from Zotero
  generated/instances/<phenomenon>.csv   # generated, never hand-edited
  generated/coverage.csv                 # generated coverage audit
  source/                                # open texts, fetched by script (not committed if large)
  LICENSES.md
work/
  candidates/<book>-<chapter>.md         # sweep output, pre-assessment
schemas/                                 # JSON Schemas for all content
scripts/                                 # Python generators and validators
site/                                    # Astro site
.claude/commands/                        # slash commands
CLAUDE.md
METHODS.md
```

### 9.2 Entry format

YAML frontmatter holds Layers 1 and 2 and the assessment; the Markdown body holds Layer 3 commentary.

```yaml
id: john-1-1
ref: "John 1:1"
kind: entry                  # entry | control
phenomena: [anarthrous-predicate-theos]
status: draft                # candidate | draft | reviewed | verified
tier: C                      # verification tier, §10.1

layer1:
  source:
    edition: WH              # edition quoted
    text: "..."
    nwt_base_text: WH
    variants: []             # readings by edition, if they differ
    difference_type: translational   # textual | translational | none
  morphological_gloss: "..."
  semantic_range: "..."
  nwt:
    - edition: "1950"
      rendering: "..."
      evidence: {type: scan|jw.org|print, ref: "...", checked_by: "...", date: "..."}
  comparisons:
    - version: NRSVue
      rendering: "..."
  consistency_ref: anarthrous-predicate-theos   # links to generated table
  wt_publications:
    - source: "bib-key"
      summary: "paraphrase of what it says"
      use: rationale         # rationale | proof-text | change-explanation

assessment:
  triggers: [T2]
  L: L2
  T: follows-base
  C: generated
  R: R3
  confidence: medium

layer2:
  strongest_case_for_nwt: "..."
  strongest_case_against_nwt: "..."
  positions:
    - label: "..."
      holders: ["..."]
      argument: "..."
      sources: ["bib-key"]
  landscape: open            # consensus | majority-minority | open
  other_translations: "..."

provenance:
  discovered_by: [A, B]      # discovery streams, §6.1
  ai:
    model: "..."
    command: "/draft-entry"
    prompt_version: "..."
  verified_by: null
  verified_on: null
```

### 9.3 Generated data

- Instance tables store only the rendering of the target word or phrase at each verse, not the full verse.
- Generated files carry a header naming the script and its inputs, and are rebuilt by CI. Hand edits are rejected.

## 10. Verification

### 10.1 Tiers

| Tier | Applies to | Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| A | Straightforward lexical or textual observation | One reviewer confirms every Layer 1 fact against its source. |
| B | Contested rendering | Tier A, plus two independent analyses (one may be AI-assisted) and a human check of all evidence. |
| C | Major doctrinal controversy | Tier B, plus textual apparatus, full panel comparison, Watch Tower defense, published scholarship on both sides, and both strongest-case sections. |

### 10.2 Status lifecycle

`candidate` → `draft` → `reviewed` → `verified`

- An entry cannot be `verified` while any citation is `unverified`, any NWT rendering lacks evidence, or any required field is empty. CI enforces this.
- Agreement between the AI and the author is not verification.

### 10.3 NWT text

- Every NWT rendering must be entered or confirmed by a person with the source in front of them (jw.org, a printed edition, or a photo), and the evidence recorded.
- Agents never supply NWT text from memory.

### 10.4 Blind scoring (when feasible)

For Tier C entries, the L dimension may be scored with the translations' identities hidden, then un-blinded. The procedure used is recorded in the entry.

### 10.5 Changes after publication

Any change to an assessment after verification is logged in the entry's history with the reason.

## 11. AI use

- AI is used to find, extract, draft, format and argue both sides. It is never the final judge of inclusion or assessment.
- AI output is always a draft, labeled `ai-draft` until reviewed.
- Every entry records the model, command and prompt version used (§9.2).
- Prompts and commands are versioned in the repository.
- Known AI failure modes the process guards against: overstated consensus, invented citations, conflated editions, confusing lexical possibility with contextual probability, and repeating apologetic arguments from any side as fact.

## 12. Contributions

Contributions are welcome through GitHub issues and pull requests.

- **What contributors can do:** submit NWT renderings with evidence; photograph older printed editions; suggest candidates; look up comparison translations; check citations; review Watch Tower's defense for fairness; review Greek and Hebrew analysis.
- **Rendering submissions** must include the verse, the edition, the text, and a source (jw.org link, or a photo cropped to the verse). A second person confirms it before it is marked checked.
- **Quotes attach to entries.** Bulk transcription of any copyrighted translation is not accepted.
- **Conduct:** discuss arguments, not people. Submissions arguing for or against any faith are welcome as positions if they are sourced.
- Final inclusion and assessment decisions follow this document, not majority vote.

## 13. Copyright

Not legal advice. The working policy:

- The NWT and most modern translations are copyrighted. They are quoted per verse, for commentary, with attribution.
- The repository never contains the bulk text of any copyrighted translation.
- Generated instance tables contain only the rendered word or phrase at each occurrence.
- Photos of printed editions are cropped to the verse or kept in a private archive, with only the transcription published.
- Open-licensed texts and datasets are used under their licenses, recorded in `data/LICENSES.md`.

## 16. Workflow and tooling

- **Authoring:** VS Code with Claude Code. Project rules in `CLAUDE.md`. Repeatable tasks as slash commands in `.claude/commands/`.
- **Queue:** GitHub issues, one per chapter sweep and one per entry or phenomenon. Agent output arrives as pull requests labeled `ai-draft`.
- **Build:** GitHub Actions validates schemas, runs generators, checks citations resolve, builds the Astro site with Pagefind search, and deploys to GitHub Pages.
- **Bibliography:** Zotero, exported to `data/bibliography.json`.
- **Comments:** Giscus (GitHub Discussions) per entry.
